'''TC Energy Corporation''' (formerly '''TransCanada Corporation''') is a Canadian [[energy company]] headquartered in [[Calgary]], Alberta and serving North America. The company's focus is fossil fuel pipelines, and it builds and operates energy infrastructure across Canada, the United States, and Mexico, with core business segments in Natural Gas Pipelines, Power Generation and Energy Storage.

TC Energy's natural gas pipeline network spans approximately {{convert|94,000|km|mi|0|abbr=off}}, transporting over 30% of the natural gas consumed across North America. The company also holds ownership interests in seven power generation facilities with a combined capacity of 4,650 megawatts (MW), including nuclear and natural gas-fired assets. 

In addition to its pipeline and power operations, TC Energy maintains strategic investments in energy infrastructure that support liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports to global markets.

The company was founded in 1951 in Calgary.<ref>{{cite book |first=William |last=Kilbourn|author-link=William Kilbourn |year=1970 |title=Pipeline: TransCanada and the Great Debate |url=https://archive.org/details/pipelinetranscan00kilb |url-access=registration | page= [https://archive.org/details/pipelinetranscan00kilb/page/29 29]|publisher=Toronto, Clarke, Irwin }}</ref> The company's US headquarters is located in the [[TC Energy Center]] skyscraper in [[Houston]], Texas.<ref name="ReferenceB">{{Cite web |last=Energy |first=T. C. |title=Iconic Houston building renamed: TC Energy Center |url=https://www.tcenergy.com/stories/2019/2019-10-15-a-significant-milestone-for-our-us-headquarters/ |access-date=2022-04-01 |website=www.tcenergy.com |language=en}}</ref>

TC Energy is the largest [[shareholder]] in, and owns the [[general partner]] of, [[TC PipeLines]].

==History==
[[File:TransCanada Logo.svg|thumb|TC Energy was known as TransCanada before rebranding in 2019|alt=]]
The company was incorporated in 1951 by a Special Act of Parliament as Trans-Canada Pipe Lines Limited.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/transcanada-pipelines-limited-history/|title=History of TRANSCANADA PIPELINES LIMITED – FundingUniverse|website=www.fundinguniverse.com|language=en|access-date=2018-03-13}}</ref> In 1954 [[N. Eldon Tanner]], president of Merrill Petroleums and former Alberta legislator, became president of the company.<ref name="LDSbio">{{cite news | url=https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1972/11/president-n-eldon-tanner-a-man-of-integrity?lang=eng |title=President N. Eldon Tanner: A Man of Integrity|author=Hugh B. Brown|publisher=LDS Church|date=November 1972|page=13|work=[[Ensign (LDS magazine)|Ensign]]|author-link=Hugh B. Brown}}</ref> The purpose of the company was to develop the [[TransCanada pipeline]] (now known as the Canadian Mainline) to supply eastern Canadian markets with natural gas produced in the west.

In 1998, TransCanada Pipelines merged with [[NOVA Corporation]]'s pipeline business, keeping the TransCanada name and becoming "the fourth largest energy services company in North America".<ref name="Merger">{{Cite web |title=TransCanada, Nova Complete Merger|url=https://www.naturalgasintel.com/articles/92796-transcanada-nova-complete-merger|publisher=Natural Gas Intelligence|date=1998-07-06|access-date=2020-03-01}}</ref>

Seeking to expand its presence in the United States, in 2016, TransCanada acquired [[Columbia Pipeline Group]] (CPG) for US$13 billion from [[NiSource]]'s Shareholders. The CPG acquisition added a pipeline network in [[Pennsylvania]] and surrounding states, where the [[Marcellus Formation|Marcellus]] and [[Utica Shale|Utica shale]] gas formations are located.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://business.financialpost.com/commodities/energy/transcanada-corp-to-acquire-columbia-pipeline-group-in-us13-billion-deal|title=TransCanada Corp to overhaul its business in US$13B acquisition of Columbia Pipeline Group|date=2016-03-18|work=Financial Post|access-date=2018-03-15|language=en-US}}</ref>

In May 2019, the company changed its name from TransCanada Corporation to TC Energy Corporation to better reflect the company's business, which includes pipelines, power generation and energy storage operations in Canada, the United States and Mexico.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-transcanada-results-idUSKCN1S911H|title=Pipeline company TransCanada changes name to TC Energy|last=Williams|first=Nia|date=2019-05-03|work=Reuters|access-date=2019-05-05|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://o.canada.com/commodities/not-a-brand-change-new-name-same-pipeline-challenges-for-tc-energy/wcm/65c5dc2d-2f5b-4664-be29-64b476d129de|title='Not a brand change': New name, same pipeline challenges for TC Energy|date=2019-05-03|website=canada.com|access-date=2019-05-05}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.commondreams.org/news/2019/05/04/transcanada-can-change-its-name-say-opponents-keystone-xl-will-never-see-light-day|title=TransCanada Can Change Its Name, Say Opponents, But Keystone XL Will Never See 'The Light of Day'|last=Queally|first=Jon|date=2019-05-04|website=Common Dreams|access-date=2019-05-05}}</ref>

In October 2019, the 56-story Bank of America Center skyscraper in [[Houston]], Texas was renamed as [[TC Energy Center]] and serves as the company's US headquarters.<ref name="ReferenceB"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Jeffrey |first=Jeff |date=October 15, 2019 |title=Bank of America Center no more: Downtown Houston tower gets new name |url=https://www.bizjournals.com/houston/news/2019/10/15/bank-of-america-center-no-more-downtown-houston.html |access-date=March 31, 2022 |website=Bizjournals}}</ref>

On October 1, 2024, TC Energy completed the spinoff of its Liquids Pipelines business creating an independent company, South Bow, focused on crude oil pipelines.

==Operations==
===Natural gas pipelines===
TC Energy's natural gas pipelines business builds, owns and operates a network of natural gas pipelines across North America that connects gas production to interconnects and end use markets. The company transports over 30% of continental daily natural gas demand through approximately 94,000&nbsp;km (58,409&nbsp;mi) of pipelines. In addition, the company owns 532 Bcf of natural gas storage facilities, making TC Energy one of the largest natural gas storage providers in North America. This segment is TC Energy's largest segment, generating approximately 87% of the company's [[EBITDA]] in 2024. The Natural Gas Pipelines business is split into three operating segments: Canadian Natural Gas Pipelines, U.S. Natural Gas Pipelines, and Mexico Natural Gas Pipelines.<ref name="2018 Annual Report">{{cite report |url=https://www.tcenergy.com/globalassets/pdfs/investors/tc-annual-report.pdf |title=Annual Report 2018 |date=2019 |access-date=March 31, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200331230257/https://www.tcenergy.com/siteassets/pdfs/investors/tc-annual-report.pdf |archive-date=March 31, 2020 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The major pipeline systems include:

*'''NGTL System (24,631&nbsp;km)''' A wholly owned subsidiary, NOVA Gas Transmission Ltd., connects gas producers in the [[Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin]] with consumers and exports.<ref>{{Cite web |title=NGTL System |url=https://www.tcenergy.com/operations/natural-gas/ngtl-system/ |publisher=TC Energy |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200309000228/https://www.tcenergy.com/operations/natural-gas/ngtl-system/ |archive-date=2020-03-09|access-date=2020-03-09}}</ref> TC Energy has the largest and most extensive natural gas network in Alberta.<ref name=pri>{{cite news|title=TransCanada rule change raises ire of producers|agency=Financial Post|last=Morgan|first=Geoffrey|newspaper=Saskatchewan StarPhoenix|url=https://www.pressreader.com/canada/saskatoon-starphoenix/20171101/282226600986366|date=November 1, 2017}}</ref>
*'''[[TransCanada pipeline|Canadian Mainline]] (14,082&nbsp;km)''' This pipeline serves as a long haul delivery system transporting natural gas from the Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin across Canada to Ontario and Québec to deliver gas to downstream Canadian and U.S. markets. The pipeline has evolved accommodate additional supply connections closer to its markets. The mainline is over 60 years old<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.transcanada.com/en/operations/natural-gas/canadian-mainline/|title=TransCanada Canadian Mainline – Overview|website=www.transcanada.com}}</ref>
*'''[[Columbia Gas Transmission|Columbia Gas]] (18,768&nbsp;km)''' This natural gas transportation system serves the [[Appalachian Basin]], which contains the [[Marcellus Formation|Marcellus]] and [[Utica Shale|Utica]] plays, two of the largest natural gas shale plays in North America. The system also interconnects with other pipelines that provide access to the U.S. Northeast and the Gulf of Mexico.
*'''[[ANR Pipeline|ANR Pipeline System]] (15,075&nbsp;km)''' This pipeline system connects supply basins and markets throughout the U.S. Midwest, and south to the Gulf of Mexico. This includes connecting supply in Texas, Oklahoma, the Appalachian Basin and the Gulf of Mexico to markets in Wisconsin, Michigan, Illinois and Ohio. In addition, ANR has bi-directional capability on its Southeast Mainline and delivers gas produced from the Appalachian basin to customers throughout the Gulf Coast Region.
*'''[[Columbia Gulf Transmission|Columbia Gulf]] (5,419&nbsp;km)''' — This pipeline system was originally designed as a long haul delivery system transporting supply from the Gulf of Mexico to major demand markets in the U.S. Northeast. The pipeline is now transitioning to a north-to-south flow and expanding to accommodate new supply in the Appalachian Basin and its interconnects with Columbia Gas and other pipelines to deliver gas to various Gulf Coast markets.
*'''Mexico Pipeline Network (3,500&nbsp;km)''' — This consists of a network of natural gas pipelines in Mexico.
*'''Southeast Gateway Pipeline (715 km)''' — A marine pipeline that will transport natural gas, connecting the supply from Tuxpan, Veracruz, to delivery points in Coatzacoalcos, Veracruz, and in Paraíso, Tabasco.
*'''Coastal Gaslink Pipeline Project (670 km)''' – Coastal Gaslink delivers natural gas from the Dawson Creek area to a facility near Kitimat, where LNG Canada prepares it for export to global markets by converting the gas to a liquefied state—also known as liquefied natural gas (LNG).
===Energy===
[[File:Bruce-Nuclear-Szmurlo.jpg|right|thumb|[[Bruce Nuclear Generating Station]]]]
TC Energy's Energy division consists of power generation and unregulated natural gas storage assets. The power business consists of approximately 4,650 megawatts (MW) of generation capacity owned or under development. These assets are located primarily in Canada and are powered by natural gas, nuclear, and wind.<ref name="2018 Annual Report"/>
*'''Western Power''' These assets include approximately 1,000 MW of power generation capacity through four natural gas-fired cogeneration facilities in Alberta and one in Arizona.
*'''Eastern Power''' These assets include approximately 2,900 MW of power generation capacity in Eastern Canada.
*'''[[Bruce Power]]''' This operates the [[Bruce Nuclear Generating Station]] in Ontario. Comprising eight nuclear units with a combined capacity of approximately 6,400 MW, it is currently the largest operating nuclear power plant in the world. TC Energy holds a 48.4% interest in the asset.
*'''Saddlebrook Solar + Storage''' project in [[Aldersyde, Alberta]] consisting of 81 MW of solar generation with utility-scale energy storage.
TC Energy has proposed two [[Pumped-storage hydroelectricity|pumped storage]] projects to store and supply clean energy. The Ontario Pumped Storage Project<ref>{{Cite web |last=Energy |first=T. C. |title=Overview — Ontario Pumped Storage Project |url=https://www.ontariopumpedstorage.com/ |access-date=2022-03-30 |website=www.ontariopumpedstorage.com |language=en}}</ref> near [[Meaford, Ontario]] would provide 1000 MW of clean energy and the proposed Canyon Creek Pumped Storage Project near [[Hinton, Alberta]] would provide 75 MW of clean energy.<ref name="tcenergy.com">{{Cite web |title=TC Energy — OntarioPumped Storage Project |url=https://www.tcenergy.com/operations/power/pumped-storage-project/ |access-date=2022-03-30 |website=www.tcenergy.com |language=en}}</ref><ref name="Energy">{{Cite web |last=Energy |first=T. C. |title=TC Energy — Canyon Creek Pumped Hydro Energy Storage Project |url=https://www.tcenergy.com/operations/power/canyon-creek-pumped-storage/ |access-date=2022-03-30 |website=www.tcenergy.com |language=en}}</ref> 

==Ownership==
As of September 2025, 84% of the share capital of TC Energy is owned by [[institutional investors]]. The dominant shareholder is the [[Royal Bank of Canada]], which owns over 12% of the company. The top 10 shareholders hold ~40% of total shares outstanding.

==Political activities==
A former TC Energy executive, in an internal meeting for external relations staff, claimed that the firm had played a central role in excluding pipelines from the scope of provincial legislation in British Columbia, Canada.<ref>{{Cite web |last=June 27 |first=Matt Simmonsand Mike De Souza This article is part of a series about revelations from leaked recordings of TC Energy executives Subscribe to our newsletter for more on this investigation |last2=Read |first2=2024 <img Height="15" Width="15" Class="inline-Icon" Alt="clock" Src="https://Thenarwhal ca/Wp-Content/Themes/the-Narwhal/Assets/Icons/Time-Outline svg" Aria-Hidden="true" /> 16 Min |date=2024-06-27 |title=‘Remarkable results’: Inside TC Energy exec’s claims of political influence |url=https://thenarwhal.ca/tc-energy-leak-investigation/ |access-date=2025-07-07 |website=The Narwhal |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Cecco |first=Leyland |date=2024-06-27 |title=British Columbia investigates claims energy company ‘dramatically’ influenced climate policy |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/article/2024/jun/27/british-columbia-energy-company-climate-policy-canada |access-date=2025-07-07 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> A TC Energy representative described the claims as exaggerated and untrue. 

In 2019 TC Energy aided the drafting of anti-protest legislation in South Dakota. The legislation, which Governor [[Kristi Noem]] signed into law in March 2019, created a fund to cover the costs of policing pipeline protests, and was accompanied by another law which sought to raise revenue for the fund by creating [[civil penalties]] for advising, directing, or encouraging persons participating in rioting. In response to the law Noem was sued by the [[Indigenous Environmental Network]], [[Sierra Club]], and other groups, who argued the laws violate [[First Amendment rights]] by incentivizing the state to sue protesters.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://theintercept.com/2019/05/23/pipeline-protest-laws-louisiana-south-dakota/|title=Pipeline Opponents Strike Back Against Anti-Protest Laws|date=May 23, 2019|accessdate=March 24, 2021|work=[[The Intercept]]|first=Alleen|last=Brown}}</ref>
